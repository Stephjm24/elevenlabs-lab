The Voice Design Zoo

I gave ElevenLabs one sentence about a character, such as a lighthouse keeper alone for forty years, a GPS that has given up or a sourdough starter that will outlive us all, and asked it to design a voice. Then I saved each voice and had it read the same line again, to see whether the character survived.

Listen to the zoo

What I wanted to know

  • Can a description give a voice a character, or only an age and an accent?
  • Does the character survive when I reuse the voice?
  • Can a sourdough starter talk on video?

What I tested

Voice design. For each character I wrote a description and a line that suits it. ElevenLabs’ voice designer (eleven_ttv_v3) made three previews of the line, and I saved one to my voice library.

Voice reuse. I then had each saved voice read the same line through a speech node (eleven_v4). The speech node only sees the line, not the description, so this shows what the voice keeps once the instructions are gone.

Trying to break it. Three prompts were built to fail: a voice that doesn’t match its script, a voice that isn’t human, and a delivery that can’t physically happen.

Talking video. I gave two characters, one human and one not, a picture and their speech, and tried two talking-head models on each.

Two of them on video

What I noticed

What worked

A single sentence really does carry character into the design preview. The ancient tree speaks at about 10 characters a second with long silences, the dragon growls low, and the GPS stays flat and defeated. Each design costs only the length of its line, about 200 credits for three previews.

On video, the result depended on what was talking. For the lighthouse keeper, Aurora gave the cleaner picture and the tighter lip-sync, and OmniHuman gave the more lifelike performance. For the sourdough starter, only OmniHuman brought it to life.

Where it broke

Saved voices lose the extremes. Reading the same line, the saved voices drift towards the middle:

  • Slow characters speed up. The sourdough starter reads its line in 20 seconds instead of 33.
  • Deep voices rise. The dragon goes from about 53 Hz to 97 Hz, almost an octave.
  • The monotone GPS gets more expressive.

The slowness and the growl were in the description, and the speech node never sees the description. The voice keeps its sound but forgets its delivery.

Aurora froze on the sourdough starter. For 20 seconds the frame stayed 95% identical to the still picture. A face made of bubbles behind glass doesn’t seem to count as a face, and it still cost about 17,000 credits.

Smaller breaks.

  • One voice design failed with a server error and worked on an identical retry.
  • A flow node pointed at a take that no longer existed until I pinned the real one.
  • One batch of speech was generated twice by accident.
  • My work laptop’s network blocked ElevenLabs’ media, so all my listening happened on my phone.

The full list is in the incident log.

What I’d try next

  • Put the delivery back into the line itself, with pauses and audio tags, and see if the saved tree slows down again.
  • Give OmniHuman a short direction prompt, and try a lip-sync model built for cartoons and animals on the non-human characters.
  • Animate the rest of the zoo with OmniHuman.

Notes on working with flows

  • One audio clip can drive several videos. On a flow, a single speech clip can feed more than one node. I connected one speech take to different pictures and models, so one central piece of audio powered several videos at once. That’s how the side-by-side comparisons were built.
  • Running a node can run everything before it. If you click run on a node further down the flow, such as the one that produces the final audio or video, it can also run the nodes that feed into it. Check exactly what you’re about to run before you press it, or you can spend credits regenerating things you already have.
  • Check My Voices before building. Before wiring voices into a flow, check My Voices to make sure every voice you saved is there and enabled.
  • Still to find out: is there a way to collect everything? I gathered the voices, clips and videos together by hand. I’d like to know whether ElevenLabs has a collection or folder option that keeps a project’s pieces in one place automatically. I’ll look into it as I learn more.
