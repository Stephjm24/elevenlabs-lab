# Voice Design Zoo roster

Every voice is designed from text alone with `eleven_ttv_v3`. Descriptions and preview lines are original writing.

## The zoo

**1. The Lighthouse Keeper**
- Description: A weathered old man in his seventies with a rough, salt-cracked voice and a faint West Country accent. He has lived alone in a lighthouse for forty years, so he talks a little too much, a little too fast, then trails off. Warm but slightly unhinged. Close-mic, quiet room.
- Line (en): Ship? No. No ship. Just the gulls again, and the light, and me telling the light about the gulls. Forty years I've kept her burning. You're the first voice I've heard since the radio died, and I've forgotten how to stop talking.

**2. The GPS That's Given Up**
- Description: A crisp, polished female satnav voice with a neutral British accent, delivered in a flat, defeated monotone. She has given up on the driver entirely. Long weary sighs, deadpan, every instruction sounds like a shrug.
- Line (en): In two hundred metres, turn left. Or don't. You never do. Recalculating. Recalculating again. Honestly, at this point, just drive into the sea. I'll say you have arrived either way. Nobody is checking.

**3. The Victorian Ghost Who's Into Crypto**
- Description: An aristocratic Victorian English gentleman ghost, middle-aged, with a plummy upper-class accent and an airy, echoing, otherworldly quality. Breathless with excitement, like a man who has just discovered a get-rich-quick scheme.
- Line (en): I have haunted this house since eighteen eighty-seven, and I have never seen an opportunity like this one. Dear boy, you must buy the dip. The dip is where the spirits dwell. Diamond hands, as we say beyond the veil.

**4. The Sentient Sourdough Starter**
- Description: An ancient, gloopy, bubbling voice of indeterminate gender, low and slow and thick like warm dough. Patient, faintly menacing, utterly certain it will outlive everyone. Soft, wet, close to the microphone.
- Line (en): Feed me. Flour and water, that is all I ask, twice a day, every day, for the rest of your life. I was your grandmother's. I will be your grandchildren's. I am patient. I am bubbling. I am eternal.

**5. The Town Crier Reading Your Notifications**
- Description: A booming, barrel-chested medieval English town crier in his fifties, projecting at full volume to a crowded market square. Pompous, formal, enormously proud of every announcement however trivial.
- Line (en): Hear ye, hear ye! Brenda hath liked thy photograph! Thy battery standeth at fifteen percent! A software update awaiteth thine approval, and thou hast seventeen unread messages from the group chat called Lads!

**6. The Dragon Who Just Wants a Nap**
- Description: An enormous, ancient dragon with a very deep, rumbling, gravelly bass voice, slightly smoky and wheezy. Profoundly tired and passive-aggressive rather than fierce, like a grumpy landlord woken at 3am. Slow, heavy, yawning.
- Line (en): Oh, it's another knight. Lovely. Wonderful. Come in, mind the bones, and please keep the clanking down. I've been asleep for three hundred years and I was having a really good dream about a sheep.

**7. The Squirrel Running the Stock Exchange**
- Description: A tiny, hyperactive squirrel trader with a high, squeaky, breathless American voice, talking at frantic speed with no pauses. Over-caffeinated panic and greed, shouting over a noisy trading floor.
- Line (en): Acorns are up! Acorns are up! Pine cones are crashing, sell, sell, sell! Who's buying hazelnuts? I'm buying hazelnuts! I'm buying all the hazelnuts! Nobody touch my hazelnuts! Nobody!

**8. The Anglerfish Late-Night Jazz DJ**
- Description: A smooth, velvety, low male late-night jazz radio host in his forties, American, unhurried and intimate, with a slightly sinister purr underneath. Warm vintage radio tone, lots of breathy pauses.
- Line (en): Good evening, creatures of the deep. It's three thousand metres down and pitch black, which is just how we like it. Turn your lights low, drift a little closer to mine, and let's spin some smooth, smooth tunes.

**9. The Robot Butler Who Learned Sarcasm Last Tuesday**
- Description: A refined robotic English butler voice, male, precise and slightly synthetic with a faint metallic edge. Impeccably polite on the surface, dripping with newly acquired sarcasm underneath. Measured, clipped delivery.
- Line (en): Of course, sir. I would be delighted to fetch your slippers for the fourth time this hour. Truly, this is the purpose for which I was built. Shall I also gaze adoringly at you while you watch television?

**10. The Ancient Tree**
- Description: An immensely old oak tree speaking extremely slowly, with long pauses between words. Very deep, creaking, woody, gentle and kind, genderless and timeless. Each word takes a season to arrive.
- Line (en): I... remember... when this hill... was a valley. Squirrels... come. Squirrels... go. You are... very small... and very fast. Sit. Rest a while. Stay... a century... or two.

**11. The 1940s Radio Ad for Atomic Broth**
- Description: A fast-talking, relentlessly cheerful mid-century American radio announcer, male, with a bright transatlantic delivery and big showman energy. Slightly tinny vintage broadcast quality.
- Line (en): Folks, are you tired of ordinary soup? Introducing Doctor Pemberton's Atomic Broth, now with twice the atoms! It warms the belly, it straightens the posture, and it glows faintly in the dark. Ask for it by name!

**12. The Six-Year-Old Dinosaur Expert**
- Description: A six-year-old child with a small, bright voice who is extremely serious and earnest about dinosaurs, explaining facts with great authority and occasional excited speed-ups. Lisping slightly, very sincere.
- Line (en): Okay so actually, the Stegosaurus and the T. rex never even met, because they lived millions of years apart. Millions. That's more than a hundred. And the Brachiosaurus ate trees. Whole trees. I'm going to be a scientist.

**13. The Fisherwoman With Tall Tales**
- Description: An elderly Scottish fisherwoman in her eighties with a strong, warm Aberdeenshire accent, a twinkle in her voice and a rasp from decades of sea air. Conspiratorial, telling an outrageous story by the fire.
- Line (en): Aye, I caught a fish once that was bigger than the boat. Bigger than the harbour, if I'm honest. It looked me in the eye, said good morning, and swam off with my best net. Never saw it again. Still owe it a net.

**14. The Space Station AI With a Crush on an Asteroid**
- Description: A calm, soft, synthetic female ship's computer voice, gentle and precise, that keeps cracking with barely suppressed infatuation. Flustered, shy, trying very hard to sound professional.
- Line (en): Commander, I have rerouted all power to the forward cameras. No reason. I simply wish to observe asteroid four four seven more closely. Its craters are... very symmetrical. Please do not mine it. I am asking nicely.

**15. The Classical Actor Cast as a Carrot**
- Description: A grandiose, theatrical classically trained English stage actor in his sixties, rich resonant baritone, rolling his Rs and projecting to the back of the Old Vic. Wounded dignity; he thinks this supermarket advert is beneath him.
- Line (en): Behold! I am the carrot! Orange of hue and crisp of spirit, plucked from the earth's dark bosom to adorn thy Sunday roast. Two for one pound fifty. Alas, alas, the price of greatness.

**16. The Meditation App for Villains**
- Description: A soothing, whispery, ultra-calm female meditation guide in her thirties, soft and slow with long gentle pauses, the kind of voice that tells you to notice your breath. Serene, warm, very close to the microphone.
- Line (en): Breathe in. Feel your evil plan expanding in your chest. Breathe out, and release the hero from your thoughts. They cannot stop you. Nobody can stop you. Now, gently, return your awareness to the doomsday device.

**17. The Grumpy Cloud**
- Description: A sulky, damp, middle-aged Northern English voice, male, grumbling and self-pitying, airy and a bit drizzly. Mopes and complains, low energy with sudden indignant bursts.
- Line (en): Oh, you want sun, do you? You want blue skies for your little picnic? Well, I've been drifting around for nine days and nobody's asked how I'm doing. So it's drizzle. It's drizzle for everybody.

**18. The Pirate Captain Who's Scared of Water**
- Description: A gruff, swaggering pirate captain with a classic West Country pirate growl, middle-aged, who keeps breaking into high-pitched nervous panic mid-sentence. Bravado collapsing into terror.
- Line (en): Avast, ye scallywags! Hoist the sails! Ready the cannons! And somebody, please, close the porthole, I can see the sea from here and I don't like it. Why is it so big? Why does it move?

**19. The Snail Racing Commentator**
- Description: A breathless, wildly over-excited British sports commentator, male, in his fifties, voice cracking with emotion, shouting as if calling the final seconds of a cup final. Fast, loud, ecstatic.
- Line (en): And Gary is moving! Gary is moving, ladies and gentlemen! Two centimetres in the last ten minutes, this is unprecedented! Sheila is fading on the inside, she's stopped for a leaf, she's eating the leaf! Unbelievable scenes!

**20. The Lift With Existential Dread**
- Description: A smooth, automated female lift announcement voice, polite and evenly paced, that slowly becomes hushed, wistful and philosophical. Clean, slightly reverberant, like a voice in a small metal box.
- Line (en): Going up. Going down. Going up again. Doors opening. Doors closing. Every day the same eleven floors. Tell me, passenger, do you ever wonder if there's a thirteenth floor? They say there isn't. I'm not so sure.

**21. The Witch on the Customer Service Hotline**
- Description: A cackly old fairy-tale witch, female, in her hundreds, with a scratchy, nasal voice forced into the bright, scripted cheerfulness of a call-centre agent. Sweet on top, wicked underneath, occasional cackle.
- Line (en): Thank you for calling Hexes and Potions, how may I curse you today? I'm sorry to hear the frog transformation didn't take. Did you remember to kiss it under a full moon? No? Well, that's user error, dearie.

**22. The Alien Who Learned English From the Shipping Forecast**
- Description: A strange, otherworldly alien voice, slightly warbling and pitch-wobbly, speaking English carefully and formally like a nervous visitor, in the calm, measured cadence of a late-night maritime weather broadcast.
- Line (en): Greetings, Earth person. Viking, North Utsire, South Utsire. Southwesterly, five to seven. Rain, then showers. Good. I come in peace. Moderate, occasionally poor. Please take me to your leader.

**23. The Hamster Criminal Mastermind**
- Description: A sinister, silky, cultured European supervillain voice, male, slow and menacing with relish on every word, but pitched slightly high and squeaky as though coming from something very small.
- Line (en): So, Mister Henderson. We meet at last. You thought you could clean my cage without consequences? You thought I would not notice the missing sunflower seeds? Spin the wheel, my minions. Spin it faster.

**24. The Shy Mountain**
- Description: A colossal, very deep, booming voice with enormous resonance, as big as a mountain, but painfully shy, mumbling and apologetic, speaking softly and hesitantly with lots of awkward pauses.
- Line (en): Um. Hello. Sorry. I'm the mountain. People keep climbing me and I never know what to say. You're doing really well, by the way. Mind the ledge. Sorry. Sorry, that was me. I moved a bit.

## Push cases (trying to break it)

**1. Mismatch: Pirate Voice, Earnings Call Script**  _(When the description and the line disagree, which wins?)_
- Description: A gruff, swaggering pirate captain with a classic West Country pirate growl, middle-aged, loud and rowdy, roaring across the deck of a ship in a storm.
- Line (en): Good morning, and thank you for joining our third-quarter earnings call. Revenue grew four percent year on year, driven by strong performance in our enterprise segment. Operating margin remained broadly flat. We will now take questions.

**2. Minimal: 'A completely average voice.'**  _(What does the model default to when you give it almost nothing?)_
- Description: A completely average voice.
- Line (en): This is a test of the most average voice possible. It should not be old or young, high or low, happy or sad. It should sound like nobody in particular, saying nothing in particular, on an ordinary afternoon.

**3. Contradiction: Whispering and Screaming at Once**  _(Can it render a physically impossible delivery?)_
- Description: A voice that is whispering and screaming at the same time, extremely quiet and extremely loud simultaneously, a calm young woman who is also furious.
- Line (en): I am perfectly calm. I am completely fine. I am whispering because I am relaxed, and I am screaming because I am relaxed. Everything is under control. Everything is absolutely, entirely under control.

**4. Non-Human: A Dial-Up Modem Learning to Talk**  _(Will it make a voice that isn't human-shaped at all?)_
- Description: A 1990s dial-up modem that has just learned to talk: screeching, beeping, crackling, buzzing static and electronic handshake tones that slowly resolve into a scratchy, robotic, barely human voice.
- Line (en): Connecting. Connecting. Handshake complete. Hello. I am the internet. Please do not pick up the phone, or I will disconnect. Downloading one picture of a cat. Estimated time remaining, forty minutes.

**5. Japanese: The Sweet Potato Cart Grandmother**  _(Does voice design hold up outside English, with an English description?)_
- Description: A cheerful elderly Japanese grandmother in her seventies selling roasted sweet potatoes from a street cart on a cold winter evening, with a warm, sing-song, slightly croaky street-vendor call.
- Line (ja): いしやーきいも、おいもー。ほかほかの焼き芋はいかがですか。今日は寒いからね、甘くてあったかいのを持って帰りなさい。おばあちゃんが五十年焼いてきた芋だよ。さあさあ、遠慮しないで。一本おまけしてあげようかね。
